01_The_Incident

April 4, 1975.
12 Minutes.

I was nine months old, strapped into a cardboard box on a C-5A Galaxy cargo plane—the first flight of Operation Babylift. The plane was in the air for 12 minutes when the mis-rigged rear ramp locks failed.

The entire rear section blew out, taking two of four hydraulic systems with it. The pilot somehow maneuvered that massive, crippled aircraft back toward Saigon, crash-landing in a rice paddy six miles short of the runway.

Of 314 passengers, 178 survived. I was one of them.

Sister Mary Nelle Gage had spent her life caring for children of the war. She was in her eighties, and she had 33+ boxes in her basement—adoption files, correspondence, photographs, documents from Friends For All Children.

"Connect as many adoptees as you can."

She entrusted them to me. An accidental archivist. I started carrying files around in my backpack, looking for a permanent home for these stories.
