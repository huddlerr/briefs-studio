01

The Accessibility Doctrine

Dr. Galbreath's leadership philosophy centers on a single imperative: translate complexity for operators. Science that stays in headquarters is science that fails the mission.

01 Core Principle

Translate Science for Operators

"A little less science-y and a little more operational." Make research findings actionable for commanders. Bridge academic rigor with field reality.

Origin Example

Creating layman's versions of forensic suicide reports so families and investigators could understand the evidence—his first act of "good trouble."

02 Strategic Move

Circumvent HQ Bottlenecks

"All of the things we produce essentially die at the headquarters level." Get tools directly to the field. Build demand signal from ground up, not top down.

His Words

"I work pretty hard to circumvent headquarters Army, headquarters Navy, headquarters Air Force... I am being nefarious and sneaky."

03 Priority #1

Demonstrate IPPW Value

"My greatest concern is the future of our IPPW workforce." Show leadership why embedded practitioners matter. Prove operational capacity, not just policy compliance.

His Mandate

"My first message to Army is put them back. You've been paid for them. You've been resourced for them for years and years. So put them back."

04 Deliverable

Commander-Actionable Tools

"If I knew where these problems are, I would take action." Resilience Index as compelling data. Quarterly dashboards for case management groups.

Active Initiative

Joint Base San Antonio dashboard combining response, prevention, and workforce data—the proof of concept he's taking to all 12 joint bases.

The Origin Story

"I got my first taste of making science accessible... and from that time on, I realized that's what I needed to do."

Early Career

OSI Forensic Consultant

Covered 17 Air Force bases responding to violent crime—homicide, suicide, sexual assault, child abuse. Attended autopsies, processed crime scenes, interpreted blood spatter evidence.

The Pivot: When the Philadelphia Inquirer focused national attention on military suicides, Galbreath noticed reports weren't written for families or investigators to understand. He created layman's versions—and got in trouble for it. "I got in the way of headquarters OSI's Doctor of Library Science, who believed he was the expert."

First lesson learned: "Science must serve the people."

Clinical Training

Johns Hopkins Clinic

Five years at the Johns Hopkins outpatient sex offender clinic in Baltimore. Worked with Dr. Fred Berlin, MD PhD—a pioneer in using medication to help offenders manage behavior. Provided expert court testimony nationwide.

Skill developed: Translating clinical science for courts and commanders.

Since 2007

SAPRO — 18 Years

Joined when SAPRO was only 18 months old. Witnessed the transformation from systems hostile to victims to supportive programs. Served as military deputy, civilian deputy, HQAE.

04

Leadership Style

✓ He Values

  • ✓
    Direct Communication Not bureaucracy. "Stump the Government Chump" energy.
  • ✓
    Field-Focused Solutions Not HQ-centric. Tools that reach unit commanders.
  • ✓
    Evidence-Based but Accessible Not academic. "I understand science most of the time."
  • ✓
    Strategic Subversion "Get in good trouble... then say 'Oh, whoops, didn't you want that to happen? Sorry. Later.'"

✗ He Resists

  • ✗
    "Science-y" Jargon Language that loses operators. Accessibility first.
  • ✗
    Tools That Die at HQ "All of the things we produce essentially die at the headquarters level."
  • ✗
    Non-Productive Prevention Activities "4-H clubs and Outward Bound activities" — unless you show him the research.
  • ✗
    Structural Equations "You are gonna see me start to drool and pass out." (He's only half-joking.)

"I never— I don't— I never sneak. I'm very transparent while I'm being sneaky. That's my story. I'm sticking to it."
