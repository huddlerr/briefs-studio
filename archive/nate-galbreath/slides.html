01

The Accessibility Doctrine

Dr. Galbreath's leadership philosophy centers on a single imperative: translate complexity for operators.

Science that stays in headquarters is science that fails the mission.

Four pillars define this doctrine →
01

The Accessibility Doctrine

// Four Pillars
01 Core Principle

Translate Science for Operators

"A little less science-y and a little more operational." Make research findings actionable for commanders.

Origin Example

Creating layman's versions of forensic suicide reports so families and investigators could understand the evidence—his first act of "good trouble."

02 Strategic Move

Circumvent HQ Bottlenecks

"All of the things we produce essentially die at the headquarters level." Get tools directly to the field.

His Words

"I work pretty hard to circumvent headquarters Army, headquarters Navy, headquarters Air Force... I am being nefarious and sneaky."

03 Priority #1

Demonstrate IPPW Value

"My greatest concern is the future of our IPPW workforce." Show leadership why embedded practitioners matter.

His Mandate

"My first message to Army is put them back. You've been paid for them. You've been resourced for them for years and years. So put them back."

04 Deliverable

Commander-Actionable Tools

"If I knew where these problems are, I would take action." Resilience Index as compelling data.

Active Initiative

Joint Base San Antonio dashboard combining response, prevention, and workforce data—the proof of concept he's taking to all 12 joint bases.

03

The Origin Story

// Career Arc
Early Career

OSI Forensic Consultant

Covered 17 Air Force bases responding to violent crime—homicide, suicide, sexual assault, child abuse. Attended autopsies, processed crime scenes.

The Pivot: Created layman's versions of forensic reports—and got in trouble for it. First lesson: "Science must serve the people."

Clinical Training

Johns Hopkins Clinic

Five years at the outpatient sex offender clinic. Worked with Dr. Fred Berlin, MD PhD. Provided expert court testimony nationwide. Skill developed: Translating clinical science for courts and commanders.

Since 2007

SAPRO — 18 Years

Joined when SAPRO was only 18 months old. Witnessed the transformation from systems hostile to victims to supportive programs. Served as military deputy, civilian deputy, HQAE. Survival strategy: Stay focused on mission.
