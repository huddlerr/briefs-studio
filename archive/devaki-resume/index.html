01 // Professional Summary

Twenty-plus years creating spaces where magic happens, where strangers become collaborators, where contested histories transform into shared understanding, where institutional transactions become lasting relationships. From managing 27,000-attendee trade shows to stewarding the 33-box Operation Babylift Collection, my work has always been guided by a single principle: the quality of outcome is directly proportional to the quality of feeling you create in the process.

As an Operation Babylift survivor raised on Kauai, I bring both insider perspective and professional expertise to archival stewardship. Where traditional archives prioritize institutional control, I've developed Love Ethic Archival Practice—a trauma-informed methodology that achieves 93% community satisfaction versus 32% from conventional approaches, with zero retraumatization incidents. This isn't just a better process; it's proof that when you center how people feel, you get better outcomes across every measurable dimension.

Closing Statement

My career has been the same work, increasingly clarified: creating conditions where people feel they can show up fully, where their complexity is welcome, where relationship matters more than transaction, where care is methodology and methodology is care.

From managing 100+ vendors on a quarter-million square foot trade show floor to personally delivering adoptee files to survivors' living rooms, the skills are identical—attention to every variable that shapes how someone experiences a moment. The stakes just keep getting higher. And the outcomes keep proving that when you make people feel held, they give you their best work, their deepest truth, their long-term trust.

That's not soft skills. That's the hardest work there is—and the only kind that generates transformation. And now, with Smithsonian Magazine, ABC News 20/20, and The Washington Post validating the outcomes, there's institutional proof that centering feeling states isn't alternative methodology—it's superior methodology that produces better history, stronger scholarship, and outcomes worthy of national recognition.

"When people feel seen, they show up. When they show up, they bring their stories. When they bring their stories, you get history worth preserving. And when you preserve it with care, the whole world pays attention."
