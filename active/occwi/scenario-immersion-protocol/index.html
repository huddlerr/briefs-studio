01

Executive Summary

Prevention training isn't working. Service members disengage, IPPWs feel ineffective, and behavior doesn't change despite educational efforts. Scenario Immersion Protocol (SIP) offers a fundamentally different approach - one that creates experiential conditions for genuine behavior change rather than information transmission.

The Approach

  • → 60-minute facilitated scenario experience with 4-6 participants
  • → Participants take anonymous roles in prevention-relevant scenarios
  • → Guided choice-making reveals actual behavioral patterns
  • → Structured debrief connects insights to real behavior change

Pilot Scope

  • • 2-4 week timeline / 3 IPPW partners
  • • ~8 hours Briefs Studio support per IPPW
  • • Zero new budget (already allocated)
  • • Scalable methodology for actual behavior change
02

The Knowledge Trap

Information is not behavior.

Standard training assumes a knowledge deficit - if people just knew the right thing to do, they'd do it. But behavior change doesn't work that way.

Service members already know they should intervene. They know substance abuse is harmful. Knowledge isn't the barrier.

Barrier 01 Social pressure and peer dynamics
Barrier 02 Fear of consequences or judgment
Barrier 03 Uncertainty about how to intervene effectively
Barrier 04 Disconnect between intent and action

Why This Matters Now

  • • IPPWs need tools that actually work, not more content
  • • Prevention metrics aren't moving with current approaches
  • • Zero budget impact makes this immediately testable
  • • Success creates proof of concept for behavioral training innovation

Step 01 // 60 Minutes

Briefs Studio facilitates a conversation with the IPPW to understand the local ground truth. This session is recorded and transcribed to capture the nuances of installation culture.

Step 02 // 3-4 Hours

Custom Protocol Development

Briefs Studio translates the Story Capture into a bespoke tactical experience.

  • A
    Custom Scenario: Tailored to the installation's specific unit context.
  • B
    4-6 Choice Points: Options spanning proactive, neutral, and avoidant responses.
  • C
    Narrative Map: Decision trees showing real consequences of choices.
  • D
    Facilitation Guide: Prompts and structured debrief questions.
Step 03 // 90 Minutes

Facilitator Training

Training the IPPW on facilitation approach: holding psychological safety, presenting choices without judgment, and navigating the decision tree.

Psychological Safety Neutral Delivery Debrief Mastery
Step 04 // 60 Minutes

Session Execution

4-6 service members immerse in the experience. 5 min setup, 30 min scenario play, 25 min structured debrief on observed patterns.

Theoretical Foundation

SIP is based on Sleepy Planet Protocol - a collaborative imagination methodology I've developed over several years through Briefs Studio. The core principle: humans have behavioral 'set points' (default patterns) shaped by environment, culture, and social conditioning. To change behavior, you need to: (1) make the current set point visible, (2) create conditions where alternative patterns can be explored safely, and (3) allow conscious observation of what emerges. SIP adapts this methodology specifically for prevention contexts, using group dynamics and role-based scenarios.

02

Risks & Mitigation

Facilitation Variance

Risk: Quality varies between IPPWs, experience falls flat.

Mitigation: Co-facilitation model ensures quality in pilot; structured guides provide a rigid framework.

Cultural Resistance

Risk: Members see it as "touchy-feely" and disengage.

Mitigation: Frame as tactical scenario training (familiar military concept). Use anonymous roles.

Measurement Difficulty

Risk: Difficult to measure behavior change quickly.

Mitigation: Focus on engagement and insight quality (immediate). Document anecdotal evidence.

Context Relevance

Risk: Scenarios not relevant to specific installations.

Mitigation: Story Capture process ensures 100% customization of every protocol.
